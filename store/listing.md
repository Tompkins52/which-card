# Witch Card on Google Play: the listing

Made by `tools/store_assets.py` (icon, feature graphic, six screenshots) with this text. Paste into Play Console.

**App name (30 max):** Witch Card: Which Card to Use

**Short description (80 max):** Open it at the store. It tells you which of your cards earns the most there.

**Full description (4,000 max):**

Witch Card is for the cards already in your wallet. Open it at the register and it tells you which one to use, right where you are standing.

No typing. With location on, the app finds the store you are in and pulls out the card that earns the most there, with the reason: 6% on groceries, 5% at Costco gas, 3x on dining. The rest of your wallet sits under it, ranked, so you see in one glance why.

It knows the fine print the banks do not explain well. Rotating categories and whether you activated them this quarter. Spending caps. Store cards that only work at their store. Stores that only take one network. Memberships that change a rate, like Prime at Amazon. Pick-your-own categories. Which travel site pays a bonus with which bank's card.

Compare grades your wallet at every kind of place, weak to great, and shows when one card pays extra at one company, like a 5x rideshare bonus that only counts with Lyft. Best cards shows what the whole catalog would earn at the store you are in, with yours marked. If you already hold the best card there, it says so.

Every perk on one screen. Credits, rotating categories, protections and what a point is worth, each with an Activated or Used switch and a link to the bank's own activation page.

Add cards by name, never a number. Point the camera at a card and it reads the name on the front, on your phone; the picture is dropped right after. Or search the list of 229 cards from 25 banks, or add your own.

Nothing leaves your phone. No account, no sign-in, no server of ours. Your wallet lives on your phone and in your phone's own Google backup. Where you are standing is looked up on OpenStreetMap, the open map, and is not kept anywhere.

Nobody pays for a spot. No ads, no sponsors, no referral links. The card pictures are drawn by us, not the banks' artwork, because the sites that show the real ones get paid when you click, and this app does not. No magic. Just math.

## Play Console answers

- **App access:** all functionality is available without special access (no sign-in).
- **Ads:** no.
- **Content rating:** questionnaire category Utility, Productivity, Communication or Other; No to everything. Result: Everyone.
- **Target audience:** 18 and over. Not designed for children.
- **News, COVID, government:** no. **Health:** none.
- **Financial features:** the app shows which of the person's own credit cards earns the most at a store; it does not offer, sell, originate or move money, loans, cards or any financial product. Answer "My app doesn't provide any financial features" unless the form's definitions have widened; if it insists, the closest category is "Other" personal finance information.
- **Data safety:** Location (approximate and precise) is used while the app is open, only to find the store the person is in: the phone's position is sent to the OpenStreetMap search services (overpass-api.de, overpass.private.coffee, overpass.kumi.systems, photon.komoot.io) in the request and is not stored by us or by the app beyond the stores it found, which stay on the phone for 30 days. Declare it as collected, optional (the app works without it), for app functionality, not shared for advertising, with encryption in transit (HTTPS), and that the person can turn it off in the app. Camera images are processed on the phone and discarded: not collected. No other data is collected; no analytics, no account, no advertising ID.
- **Permissions used:** Location (store lookup, optional), Camera (card scanner, optional), Internet (map lookup and card data updates).
- **Pricing on Play:** Free. The web app's "99 cents once" is not wired anywhere yet; when it is, it goes through Play Billing in the Android app (a one-time product) and the listing text changes.
