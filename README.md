# Witch Card

https://witch-card.tompkinssoftwaresystems.com

Formerly Which Card. The name is a pun on what it does: it tells you which card to use, and its mascot is a card dressed as a witch. No magic, just math.

A home-screen web app that tells you which of your credit cards to use at the store you are standing in, before you pay. Open it, confirm the store (or let it find the store from your location), and the best card is on screen. No account, no server, nothing leaves the phone except an anonymous nearby-places lookup when location is on.

Built by Tompkins Software Systems.

## How it works

The first open shows a welcome note from the mascot, a goofy cartoon card in a witch hat that flies its broom across a gold moon, trailing gold dust ("No magic. Just math."): the app is built for people who pay their statement in full and never pay interest; anyone who pays interest is welcome to carry on as they are. With no cards added yet, the Pay screen is one screen with no scrolling on any phone: the mascot parked by the moon (sized to whatever room is left, and stepping aside on a very short phone), what the app does, three one-line points, the Choose my cards button just above the tab bar, and the pledge under it.

- **Pay** is the whole point, and it fits on one screen with no scrolling. The card to use comes first: its picture with the witch hat on it, its name and the big reward with why ("4%, on gas") right under the store; tapping it opens the card. There is no amount to type: which card to use does not depend on how much you spend. Other stores near you ("Not here?") sit right under the store line. Under the answer, anything to watch (activation, caps) and a note about the store (Costco takes Visa only), then every other card in the wallet stacked best first in a stitched pocket that fills the screen down to the tab bar: every card gets at least its name, rate and why (points also show what they are worth), and the rest of the room goes by rank, the next best card the most and the worst the least, so a weak card never takes over the screen; a card with room also shows its chip and network. Each opens its card, and "Best cards" in the pocket opens the best cards for that store. When they do not all fit, the pocket keeps every card at a hand-sized height and scrolls inside itself, like thumbing through a real wallet, with the last card peeking out at the bottom; the screen itself never scrolls (`fitPay` works from layout, not from the animated positions; checked at 390 x 844, 393 x 759 and 375 x 647). Pick the store by location, search, or a kind of place; the stores near you and the kinds of place each sit on one row that scrolls sideways, so the picker stays short. Activation and perk reminders live at the top of the Wallet tab, which shows a gold dot while one is waiting.
- **Where you are standing.** With location on, the app looks up the stores around you every time it opens, and again when you come back to it after a minute or more, then shows "You're at" and the store's name with the best card for it, before any amount is typed. The stores next door are one tap away ("Not here?"). A store picked by hand in the last three minutes is kept, so switching to a payment app and back never moves you, and a new store clears the old amount. If the lookup fails, the last store comes back. The Pay screen asks once, at the top, whether to turn location on.
- **Fast lookups.** Two position requests start together, a quick one from Wi-Fi and cell towers and a precise one from GPS; the first usable fix answers (one rougher than 150 meters waits up to 4 seconds for a better one) and a clearly better or clearly different fix in the next 12 seconds corrects it. The map search reaches past the 120 meters the answer uses by as much as the fix might be off, so a better fix inside the searched area is answered again from the same list with no second lookup. Four public map services are raced: photon.komoot.io (a search index over OpenStreetMap, usually well under a second) and the Overpass servers overpass-api.de and overpass.private.coffee at once, overpass.kumi.systems two seconds later or as soon as one fails. The first answer that names a store the app knows wins; empty answers are merged and count only once every service has answered; a busy server's "timed out" reply or "at capacity" page is not an answer. "You're at" is only said when it is sure: the position is inside the place's footprint (the map's bounding box for it) or within about 30 meters of it, a little more when the fix is rough, never more than 60. Otherwise the stores near you are offered as chips, closest first with the distance in feet, and you tap the one you are at; the answer shows the everything-else card until then. A found store shows up to three other places near you under "Not here?", below the answer. Names from the map match the store list on whole words only (a bar is never "British Airways" through its "BA" alias), and merchants that are not places (airlines, websites, services) are never matched from the map. Any named shop counts as a store (everything-else rate when the app has no better rule); parking lots do not. Typing a name the app does not know guesses what kind of place it is: clues in the name first (Steakhouse, Grill, Pizza, Pharmacy, Hotel and so on, instant and offline), then a name search of the map biased to where the phone is (photon.komoot.io), which often knows a place under that name nearby even when it is missing at this spot. The guess is one tap; the other kinds sit under it for when the guess is wrong or there is none. A place the map does not know can be remembered: pick the store or its kind by hand while the phone knows where it is, and a one-line "Remember this spot?" offers to save the name at that position (`myPlaces` in the app state, carried by the setup code). From then on, standing within about 55 meters of that spot says "You're at" by that name, map or no map; More lists the spots and can forget one. After 8 seconds the screen says the map is slow and keeps looking; after 60 it gives up and opens the store search. The stores found around a spot are kept on the phone for 30 days (`whichcard.places.v2`: where the lookup was made, to about 10 meters, how far around it, and the named places found), so a place you have been before is answered with no lookup; a spot older than 3 days is refreshed quietly. The last lookup (how fast and how close the position was, what each service answered and how fast, what it found) shows under More, written as it goes, so a wrong or missing store can be reported exactly. "Test location on this phone" under More runs each step by hand (permission, position, each map service one at a time) and shows what came back; About shows the build.
- **Best cards** for a store or a kind of place: the cards that earn the most there all year, yours and the rest ("In your wallet"), ranked, with fees, caps, memberships and who can get them; rotating bonuses are left out. In a tie a card you already hold comes first and ties share a rank (1, 1, 3), and when your card is at the top a note says you already have the best card there, so nothing suggests signing up for one that only matches it. It opens from the pocket on Pay and from every scale on Compare; a card opens its detail and closing it comes back to the list. For the place you are at, it ends with the yearly math for the best card you don't have (a trips-a-year slider, prefilled from logged purchases or a typical pace, in dollars after the annual fee, with the trip count at which a fee card pays for itself); that part can be turned off under More.
- **Adding cards.** The list opens with the best-known cards first (Sapphire Preferred, Freedom Unlimited, Prime Visa, Costco Anywhere, Amex Gold and so on, in the order set by `POPULAR` in app.js), then the rest; searching or picking a bank keeps that order, and business cards always come after every personal card (business cards are hidden until you search or tap Business).
- **Scan a card.** In Add a card, the camera opens in the sheet with a card-shaped frame; hold the card in it and the app reads the letters printed on it (tesseract.js, bundled under `vendor/tesseract/`, loaded from the app's own folder the first time, about 6 MB, then kept for offline use), keeps reading frames until one card clearly stands out (usually a second or two), turns the camera off and offers it as a one-tap row; after about 9 seconds it offers the closest ones to pick from (every card tied with the best, up to six, so reading only UNITED lists every United card). Only the frame is read, so a stone counter or a patterned table around the card does not matter. Each picture goes gray, is softened a little against the fine lines pressed into metal-look cards, and every pixel is compared with the area around it, so light letters on a silver card read as well as white letters on navy and glare washes out; frames alternate between light and dark letters, and now and then a card held upright. Words the reader is not sure of count only when nothing else matched, and a card read only by short words ("it", "ink") also needs its bank's name or its whole name in a row, so the noise of a busy background never names a card. "Take a photo instead" uses the camera app (or a saved photo) and reads the whole picture; it is also the way in when the camera is blocked or missing. Cards closed to new applicants (the original Chase Freedom, Amex EveryDay) are read too. The reader is told to read letters only, never digits; frames, photos and words are dropped as soon as the name is matched; nothing is sent anywhere, and `tests/scan_check.py` fails if any request leaves the app's own server during a scan. Matching is `matchCardText` in the engine: distinctive words of the name count most, generic words little, and an issuer or network that contradicts the text counts against a card.
- **Wallet** holds the cards the person carries. Each card has a detail page: a perks checklist (every credit and perk on one screen, with an Activated or Used switch and a link to the issuer page that activates it), how it earns, this quarter's rotating categories with an activation switch and the issuer's activation page, chosen categories for pick-your-own cards, cap progress, spend goals, tips, gotchas, protections, and what a point is worth.
- **Compare** is two lists. First, how the wallet does: one line per kind of place with a scale from red (weak) through amber (good) to green (great) and a black dot where the wallet's best card sits between a plain 1% card and the best card anyone can get there (`walletScale` in the engine; this quarter's rotating bonus counts for the wallet and says when it ends; cards limited to a group or needing a membership do not set the top). Weak, Good and Great label the scales once at the top, so all twelve fit on about one screen; no card is named, and tapping a row opens Best cards for that kind of place. The dots slide into place when the tab opens. Second, the best sign-up offers for this person: ranked by what the bonus is worth to them after the first year's fee, at their goal under More (for cash back, points count at their cash value, so airline and hotel offers drop out; for points and miles, at transfer value), with cards they already hold left out. A row of banks scrolls sideways above it: pick one, or a few that share points (Chase and Citi, say); none picked means all, and the same picks apply in Best cards. Each offer opens the card, which now shows its sign-up offer and what it is worth to them, with a link to the bank's page. Last, this quarter's rotating 5% categories, one line per card. Travel as a kind of place (on Compare, in Best cards and on the Pay screen) also counts flights and hotels booked directly (`includes` on the category), so cards that pay extra there, like the Sapphire Reserve's 4x or the Platinum's 5x on flights, are not judged on their base rate; booking through a card's own travel site is its own place (Chase Travel, Amex Travel and so on). A rotating bonus not yet activated shows "activate by" on its line. When a card you hold pays extra at one company of that kind (the Reserve's 5x at Lyft or 8x through Chase Travel, a Prime Visa at Amazon, the Costco card at Costco gas), the row counts that rate with an asterisk and names the company ("* with Lyft only"), since you can choose to go there; only a bonus for the company itself counts, never one that spills over from another kind, and Everything else never gets one. A bank's travel site pays only its own bank's cards (`issuer` on the merchant). Cash+ 5% picks are fast food and movie theaters (their own categories), not all dining and entertainment; saved picks carry over.
- **More** holds the priority (cash back, points and miles, or earning a bonus), redemption overrides per program, location and phone-wallet settings, the data date, a setup code for moving to another phone, and the privacy and contact text.

The recommendation logic lives in `engine.js` and has no DOM dependencies; `tests/engine.test.js` covers caps, rotating quarters, exclusions, network acceptance, valuations and goals.

## Look

The idea is pulling the right card out of your wallet. Behind everything runs a field of fine curvy lines, like the line work printed on a card, plum with a few marigold, drifting slowly sideways and swaying a little (drawn once at start by `waves()`, two layers moved by transforms only, so it costs the phone almost nothing; still under reduced motion); the header is frosted so they show through. The card to use comes first, tilted beside its reward, a witch hat lands on its corner a beat later with a few gold sparks, the rest of the wallet tucks into a plum pocket under it (a rotating card there shows its quarter, "Activated" with a check or "Activate by Dec 14", then "5x groceries and dining", so its worth shows even where the bonus does not apply), and everything else stays quiet so the card colors carry the color. Motion is there to show what changed, and there is a lot of it now: while it finds you, rings ripple out from the pin like radar; when it does, the store's icon pops; the card to use pulls out of the wallet, its name slides in, the hat lands with a few gold sparks, a shine runs across the card face and the rate counts up and lands with a pop; the rest of the wallet is dealt into the pocket one card after another, tilting a little as they settle; stores near you pop in. Tabs ease in, the icon hops and a pill slides along the tab bar to the tab you picked; a gold dot pings on the Wallet tab when a reminder waits. On Compare, each scale fills in from the left and its dot slides into place, and offers rise in rank order. Sheets spring up from the bottom with their contents rising in, change in place (no second slide) when you open a card from a list or go back, and glide away when closed; toasts spring up and slip away. Adding a card flashes its row and pops "Added"; switches spring; the scanner frame breathes gold while it looks and flashes when it has the card. All of it stops when the phone asks for reduced motion. The witch shows up in four places only: the hat on the card to use, the header mark and home-screen icon (the mascot art), and the mascot flying across the moon on the welcome note. The icons and the flying cutout are made from icons/mascot-1024.jpg by tools/make_icons.py. Palette: Mist #F5F2F8 (background), Ink #1E1328 (text), Plum #5B2E86 (actions), Pocket #2A1640 (the wallet) and Marigold #F6A623 (wins), with a dark version of each. Type is Archivo, one variable font used wide for names and headings and condensed for rates and dollar figures. The font ships with the app (`fonts/`), so no font service is contacted. The look follows the phone's light or dark setting unless one is picked under More. Dark mode is near-black and neutral, with purple kept for actions, like a black leather wallet with gold stitching.

## Keeping the card data current

There is no public feed of credit card earning rules, so the catalog is a curated file with a source URL and a `verified` date on every entry. Two things keep it from going stale:

1. **Daily check** (`.github/workflows/data-check.yml`). Every morning the job fetches each source page, hashes the visible text, and compares it with the last run. When a page changed, a card has not been verified in 90 days, an offer in 45 days, or a rotating card has no categories on file for the quarter that starts within 30 days, it opens (or comments on) a GitHub issue labeled `data-review` with a to-do list. The job records the new hashes so each change is reported once. It never edits card data on its own; a person reads the page, fixes the number if it moved, sets `verified` to today, and commits. The next deploy carries the change to every phone.
2. **In-app refresh.** On every launch the app fetches `data/*.json` with caching disabled and keeps the newest copy in local storage, so a data commit reaches users without reinstalling. The data date shows under More, and every card detail shows its own verified date and a "report a wrong number" link.

Some bank sites block automated requests. Those pages show up under "Pages that blocked the check" so they get opened by hand.

## Running it

Static files, so any web server works. For local use:

```
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

then open http://localhost:8000. Location lookup needs HTTPS (or localhost).

Live at https://witch-card.tompkinssoftwaresystems.com (the tompkins52.github.io/which-card address forwards there). The repository keeps its old name, and so do the storage keys (`whichcard.*`), the engine's global (`WhichCardEngine`) and the setup code prefix, so nothing anyone saved is lost.

Deploy: GitHub Pages through `.github/workflows/pages.yml` (Settings > Pages > Source: GitHub Actions). The custom domain is set in Settings > Pages > Custom domain, with a Cloudflare CNAME record `witch-card` pointing at `tompkins52.github.io` (DNS only, gray cloud). No CNAME file is needed because the site deploys through Actions.

Checks before committing data:

```
python3 tools/validate_data.py
node tests/engine.test.js
```

Phone checks in a headless browser (Playwright): `python3 tests/ui_check.py` walks the app, and `python3 tests/location_check.py` fakes the phone's position and the OpenStreetMap answer to check the store lookup on open, on return, after a hand pick, and when the lookup fails. `python3 tests/scan_check.py` runs the real card reader against a pretend camera and photos from `tests/fixtures/` (drawn by `tests/fixtures/make_cards.py`: a silver card with light letters on a speckled stone counter, which the first reader could not read, and a gold card with dark letters on wood) and checks the right card is offered, the camera turns off, and nothing leaves the phone. `python3 tests/sw_check.py` assembles the site like the deploy and checks the service worker: the whole shell cached, the home-screen start address and the shortcuts working offline with the app's font, and a new deploy replacing the whole shell at once.

## Privacy

The app stores the card names the person picks, their settings and goals in the browser's local storage on that phone. Nothing is sent to a server. With location turned on, the phone's coordinates are sent to public OpenStreetMap services (photon.komoot.io, overpass-api.de, overpass.private.coffee and overpass.kumi.systems) each time the app opens at a place it has not seen in the last 30 days, to find the businesses around it. A store name you type that the app does not know is sent to photon.komoot.io, with the position when location is on, to learn what kind of place it is. The places found, and where the lookup was made to about 10 meters, stay on the phone for 30 days so a return visit is instant; turning location off or erasing the app clears them; no account or identifier goes with them. The font is part of the app, so no font service sees a visit. Scanning a card happens entirely on the phone: the reader is part of the app, it reads letters only, the camera is on only while the scan is open, and the frames or photo are dropped as soon as the card name is matched. There is no analytics, no advertising and no referral link.

## Independent

Made by a cardholder, not a bank. No sponsors. No ads, ever. No subscription, ever. 99 cents once, for life. Witch Card takes nothing from any bank: no referral links, no paid placement, no sponsored cards. Every card gets the same treatment and the ranking is arithmetic on the person's own cards. It is not affiliated with any bank or card network. Earning rates are copied from issuer pages and can change without notice; the cardholder agreement always wins. Point values are estimates.
